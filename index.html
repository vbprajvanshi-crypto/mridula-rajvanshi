01 / Her life

Remembering a remarkable life

The obituary · 1944–2026

In loving memory of a devoted wife, mother, grandmother, and a woman whose warmth, courage, and love for life touched everyone who knew her.

Mrs. Mridula Rajvanshi passed away on October 1, 2026, in Navi Mumbai, India, at the age of 81. She leaves behind a loving family, a lifetime of cherished memories, and an abundance of love that will endure for generations.

Born on December 15, 1944, in Aligarh, Uttar Pradesh, to Mr. Murari Lal Gupta and Mrs. Sharbati Devi, Mridula was the youngest of eight children. Spirited and independent from an early age, she was fearless in standing up to neighborhood bullies, yet equally fond of dressing up and embracing fashions ahead of her time. Her confidence, individuality, and enthusiasm for life remained with her throughout her years.

On May 24, 1963, she married Mr. Brahma Prakash Rajvanshi in Aligarh. Together, they raised three children: Deepshikha, Vikrant, and Vijayant. She later became a proud grandmother to five grandchildren: Rishabh, Ananya, Abhay, Aditya, and Naina.

Mridula devoted her life to providing her children with a loving home, a good education, and opportunities to develop their abilities. During the years when her husband's work frequently kept him away, she took primary responsibility for their upbringing, instilling values of independence, responsibility, and perseverance.

Her determination was tested in September 1984, when her fourteen-year-old son Vikrant suffered a serious accident. Through years of treatment, surgeries, and rehabilitation, she remained steadfast in her belief that he would recover, often believing in him more than he believed in himself. She encouraged him with two simple reminders: “If we experience all the happiness, who will bear the sorrow?” and “This, too, shall pass.” Reflecting on her devotion, Vikrant said, “All mothers give life to their children. My mother gave me more than one life.”

Mridula was happiest when surrounded by family and friends. Known for her warmth and generous hospitality, she welcomed visitors into her home and delighted in preparing elaborate home-cooked meals. Her enthusiasm for life remained strong even as advancing age and declining health imposed limitations.

She loved gardening and had a green thumb. At the family home in Lucknow, she tended a garden with more than one hundred potted plants. She was especially fond of fruit-bearing trees and was well known for planting and growing mango, lychee, pomegranate, guava, custard apple, and mulberry trees in her garden. She also loved traveling and collecting knick-knacks from the places she visited.

Deeply spiritual, she found comfort in prayer and music. She often began her mornings with prayers and could be heard humming familiar tunes around the house. For her children, the sound of their mother's singing meant that she was happy and all was well in their little world. She also carefully preserved family photographs, school report cards, and other keepsakes, quietly safeguarding memories that her family now treasures.

Her grandchildren brought her immense happiness. She often joked that the interest is dearer than the principal, expressing her belief that grandchildren were even more precious than one's own children.

For her daughter Deepshikha, Mridula was her greatest strength, closest friend, and source of comfort. She remembers her mother's sparkling eyes, radiant smile, and reassuring presence. “That was the magic of Mummy — her very presence was a promise that I was never alone.”

For her younger son, Vijayant, she was his best friend and confidante who listened without judgement. Despite living thousands of miles apart, they maintained a cherished routine of morning FaceTime calls during his commute to work. Hearing his mother's familiar voice brought him peace, while seeing her son reassured her that he was well. As Vijayant put it, “My mother was my best friend and my life's constant. Through all the ups and downs, she was always there for me. She was my 'home'.”

Mridula taught her children the importance of willpower, patience, and remaining hopeful through life's difficulties. She found her greatest fulfillment in their happiness and accomplishments and worked to keep the family close as they established lives of their own.

Her absence will be felt in countless ordinary moments: a family gathering without her, a familiar song, an old photograph, or the instinct to pick up the phone and call her.

Above all, Mridula Rajvanshi will be remembered as the woman who made her family feel loved, cared for, and at home.

Om Shanti.

A family remembrance

In loving memory of a devoted wife, mother, grandmother, and a woman whose warmth, courage, and love for life touched everyone who knew her.

Mrs. Mridula Rajvanshi passed away on October 1, 2026, in Navi Mumbai, India, at the age of 81. She leaves behind a loving family, a lifetime of cherished memories, and an abundance of love that will endure for generations.

Born on December 15, 1944, in Aligarh, Uttar Pradesh, to Mr. Murari Lal Gupta and Mrs. Sharbati Devi, Mridula was the youngest of eight children, with three brothers and four sisters. Growing up in a large family, she developed the spirited, independent personality that would remain with her throughout her life. As a young girl, she was fearless in confronting neighborhood bullies and never hesitant to stand up for herself or others. At the same time, she loved dressing up and had a keen sense of fashion, often embracing styles well ahead of her time. Her confidence, individuality, and enthusiasm for life were evident from an early age.

On May 24, 1963, she married Mr. Brahma Prakash Rajvanshi in Aligarh. Together, they built a family and raised three children: Deepshikha, born in 1965; Vikrant, born in 1970; and Vijayant, born in 1979. She later became a proud and affectionate grandmother to five grandchildren: Rishabh, Ananya, Abhay, Aditya, and Naina.

Mridula devoted her life to raising her children and providing them with a secure, comfortable, and loving home. During the years when her husband's work frequently kept him away from home, she took primary responsibility for managing the household and raising their children. She was determined that they receive a good education and have access to opportunities that would allow them to develop their interests and abilities. She believed in the importance of education, financial prudence, personal responsibility, and preparing her children to stand on their own feet.

Her determination was tested in September 1984, when her fourteen-year-old son Vikrant suffered a serious accident. The years that followed involved medical treatment, surgeries, rehabilitation, and repeated setbacks. Through it all, Mridula remained steadfast in her belief that her son would overcome the challenges before him. Whenever he struggled, she was there to help him find the strength to continue, often believing in him more than he believed in himself. She reminded him of two simple truths: “If we experience all the happiness, who will bear the sorrow?” and “This, too, shall pass.” These became guiding principles that helped him through some of the most difficult periods of his life.

Reflecting on his mother's devotion, Vikrant expressed what her presence had meant to him: “All mothers give life to their children. My mother gave me more than one life.” Having survived several life-threatening experiences during his childhood and teenage years, he understood better than most the depth of her determination to keep him safe. The experience forged an especially close bond between them, and she remained protective of him throughout her life, while continuing to care deeply for all three of her children.

For her family, Mridula was the person around whom everyday life revolved. As her children grew older and started families of their own, her love and concern for them remained unchanged. She took comfort in knowing they were happy and well, and was always there when they needed her. She wanted her family to remain connected, even as careers, responsibilities, and geographical distances made spending time together increasingly difficult. She was happiest when everyone was together.

Her affection extended well beyond her immediate family. Among relatives and friends, she was known for her warmth, generosity, and hospitality. Her home was always open to extended family and friends. She took particular pleasure in preparing elaborate home-cooked meals, often going to considerable lengths to ensure that everyone was well fed and comfortable. Food was one of the many ways she expressed her love.

Mridula had a natural enthusiasm for life. She enjoyed the company of people, family gatherings, celebrations, good food, and the pleasures of everyday living. She retained her interest in dressing well and enjoying life even as advancing age and declining health began to impose limitations. Those who knew her admired her spirit, her generosity, and her ability to remain cheerful in difficult circumstances.

She loved gardening and had a green thumb. At the family home in Lucknow, she tended a garden with more than one hundred potted plants. She was especially fond of fruit-bearing trees and was well known for planting and growing mango, lychee, pomegranate, guava, custard apple, and mulberry trees in her garden. She also loved traveling and collecting knick-knacks from the places she visited.

She was also practical and thoughtful in managing family affairs. She believed in saving for a rainy day and took considerable care in preserving things she considered important. Among her belongings, her family has since discovered carefully organized collections of birth announcements, wedding invitations, school report cards, class photographs, and other keepsakes from her children's lives. Documents that might easily have been misplaced or discarded over the decades were preserved with remarkable care. Together, they tell the story of a mother who treasured her children's lives and quietly safeguarded their memories.

Faith was another constant in Mridula's life. A deeply spiritual woman, she believed in a higher power and found comfort in prayer and music. She often began her mornings with prayers, and her voice could frequently be heard humming a familiar tune as she went about her daily activities around the house. For her children, the sound of their mother's singing was a reassuring part of growing up. It meant that she was happy, that life was proceeding as it should, and that all was well in their little world.

The arrival of her grandchildren brought her immense happiness. She delighted in their company, followed their lives with interest, and took great pride in their accomplishments. She often joked that the interest is dearer than the principal, a familiar Indian expression suggesting that grandchildren are even more precious than one's own children. In her case, there was considerable truth behind the humor. Her grandchildren occupied a special place in her heart, and she made no effort to conceal her affection for them.

For her daughter Deepshikha, Mridula was a source of strength, comfort, and inspiration throughout her life. She was her closest friend, her confidante, and the person whose presence made life's uncertainties seem less daunting. Deepshikha remembers her mother's sparkling eyes and radiant smile, and the reassurance she felt simply by being near her. No matter what difficulties life presented, knowing that her mother was there gave her the confidence that somehow, everything would be all right. She admired her mother's beauty, wisdom, kindness, and inner strength, and looked to her as an example of the woman she herself aspired to become. As Deepshikha expressed it, “That was the magic of Mummy — her very presence was a promise that I was never alone.”

For her youngest son, Vijayant, Mridula was also his closest friend and confidante. Their relationship was one of openness and mutual understanding. He could speak freely with her, knowing that she would listen without judgment. She offered advice when needed and was often the voice of reason. Although they lived thousands of miles apart, with Mridula in India and Vijayant in the United States, they maintained a simple routine that helped bridge the distance. Each morning, as he began his drive to work, he would call her on FaceTime. She could see him while he concentrated on the road, and he could hear her familiar voice. The conversations were often about ordinary things, but they brought comfort to both. For him, hearing his mother's voice was a peaceful way to begin the day. For her, seeing her son, even briefly, reassured her that he was doing well. As Vijayant put it, “My mother was my best friend and my life's constant. Through all the ups and downs, she was always there for me. She was my 'home'.”

Among the most enduring lessons Mridula passed on to her children was the importance of willpower and the belief that difficult times, however overwhelming, would eventually pass. She encouraged them to face adversity with determination, patience, and a positive outlook. These were not merely words of advice; they reflected the way she approached her own life. Her children had watched her confront hardships, often with a smile, and had learned from her example. As Vikrant later reflected, they did more than adore their mother; they revered her for the strength she demonstrated and the courage she inspired in them.

Mridula considered her children and their accomplishments to be her greatest source of pride. She found fulfillment in seeing them grow into independent adults, establish their own families, and lead meaningful lives. Their happiness mattered deeply to her, as did the knowledge that they would always have one another.

Her passing has left a profound emptiness in the lives of those closest to her. For more than six decades, she was a constant presence in her family's life: remembering birthdays and anniversaries, worrying about everyone's health and happiness, preparing favorite meals, offering advice, and making sure that no one felt forgotten. Her love was expressed as much through these ordinary acts of care as through words.

Her children and grandchildren will remember her through the stories she told, the meals she prepared, the songs she sang, the traditions she maintained, and the many small gestures that made each of them feel loved.

There will be moments when her absence is especially difficult to bear: a family gathering without her, a photograph that brings back an unexpected memory, a familiar song she would hum, or a morning drive when the instinct to call her remains, even though she can no longer answer.

Mridula Rajvanshi lived with courage, generosity, faith, and a deep love for her family. She found happiness in the happiness of those she loved and devoted herself to making their lives better.

Above all, she will be remembered as the woman who made her family feel loved, cared for, and at home.

She is deeply loved, profoundly missed, and will always remain a part of the family she held so dear.

Om Shanti.
